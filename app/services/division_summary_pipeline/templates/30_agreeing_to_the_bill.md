**Bill Timeline:** *After its second reading, a bill can be looked at in detail and its text amended. The House of Representatives calls this stage consideration in detail, and in the Senate it takes place in committee of the whole. Both chambers usually take the bill as a whole rather than part by part. When the amendments have been dealt with, the chair puts a question on the whole bill. It is "That the bill be agreed to", or "That the bill, as amended, be agreed to" if amendments were agreed to. In the Senate, a bill with no amendments agreed to is put as "That the bill stand as printed". Nobody moves this question; the chair puts it. The House of Representatives also puts it, without debate, when a bill comes back from the Federation Chamber, the House's second debating chamber. Agreeing to the question accepts the bill's text as it then stands, but does not pass the bill. That happens at the third reading, the final vote on the bill in each chamber.*
